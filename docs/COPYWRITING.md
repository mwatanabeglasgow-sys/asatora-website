# Asatora — Website Copy Playbook

A working copy guide for asatora.shop, distilled from the major authors, copywriters, and
psychologists who shaped marketing and persuasion over the last ~50 years. Use it when writing
or editing any on-site copy. It is applied first to the **Products section** (see end).

> Hard brand rules still win over every technique here. Never use the word "wellness." Never
> discount or fake an "original price." 朝虎 only. Shibushi / Kagoshima origin — never Uji.
> Single-origin, traceable, stone-milled. See the vault `CLAUDE.md`.

---

## The sources (≈ last 50 years)

**Direct-response / copywriting**
- **David Ogilvy** — *Ogilvy on Advertising* (1983). Headline does ~80% of the work; lead with the
  customer's self-interest; specifics out-sell adjectives; respect the reader's intelligence.
- **Eugene Schwartz** — *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966). **Market awareness** (unaware →
  most-aware) and **sophistication** stages: meet the reader where their knowledge already is;
  copy *channels* existing desire, it doesn't create it.
- **Joseph Sugarman** — *The Adweek Copywriting Handbook* (17 axioms). The "slippery slide" — every
  line exists to make you read the next; first job of copy is to get the first sentence read.
- **Gary Halbert** — *The Boron Letters* (1980s). Write to **one** person, like a private letter;
  "enter the conversation already in their head"; plain, human voice beats corporate.
- **Robert Collier** — *The Robert Collier Letter Book*. Enter the conversation in the reader's mind.
- **John Caples / Claude Hopkins** — test everything; concrete, measurable claims; specificity.
- **Drayton Bird, Dan Kennedy** — clarity, offer, and the reader's "what's in it for me."

**Psychology of persuasion**
- **Robert Cialdini** — *Influence* (1984) + *Pre-Suasion*. Seven levers: **reciprocity,
  commitment/consistency, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity, unity.**
- **Daniel Kahneman & Amos Tversky** — *Thinking, Fast and Slow* / Prospect Theory (1979).
  **System 1** (fast, emotional) drives most buying; **loss aversion** (~2× the pull of gain);
  **anchoring**; **framing**.
- **Rory Sutherland** — *Alchemy* (2019). Perceived/psychological value > functional value;
  reframe rather than argue; meaning is created, not just described.

**Story & brand**
- **Donald Miller** — *Building a StoryBrand* (2017). The **customer is the hero, the brand is the
  guide**; a guide shows **empathy + authority**; clarity beats cleverness.
- **Luxury / premium practice** — **understatement** (leave room for imagination), **specificity =
  sophistication**, **sensory language** (make it tangible before it's touched), confidence over hype.

## The principles we actually use

1. **Customer is the hero; Asatora is the guide.** Copy is about the reader's morning, ritual, and
   taste — not about us. Speak to one person ("you"), like a letter (Halbert/Collier).
2. **Lead with the strongest concrete idea** (Ogilvy/Caples). The headline/H2 must carry value on
   its own. Specifics ("30g, stone-milled, one garden in Shibushi") out-sell adjectives.
3. **Specificity = sophistication; write to the senses** (luxury practice). Name the garden, the
   stone, the colour of the pour. Show, don't label.
4. **Understate.** Confidence is quiet. No exclamation, no hype words, no "premium/luxury/amazing."
   Let the imagination finish the sentence.
5. **System 1 first** (Kahneman/Sutherland). Lead with feeling and image; let the rational proof
   (traceability, process) follow and reassure.
6. **Authority + empathy = trust** (Miller/Cialdini). Generational family, stone mills, single
   garden = quiet authority. Empathy = naming the ritual they already value.
7. **Social proof & provenance over claims** (Cialdini). "Traceable to one mountain," "never
   blended" reassures more than adjectives.
8. **Scarcity & loss aversion — ethically only** (Cialdini/Kahneman). Truthful scarcity is fine:
   *first harvest, single garden, small batches, the tin you keep in use.* **Never** a fake
   discount or struck-through price — that violates the no-discount brand rule. Frame around what
   stays/continues (the ritual unbroken), not "don't miss out."
9. **Commitment/consistency & unity** (Cialdini). Inclusive, identity-aligned language: people who
   already keep a morning practice; "the way the family drinks it."
10. **Clarity beats cleverness** (Miller/Sugarman). If a line is decorative but not earning the next
    line, cut it. Every sentence pulls to the next (slippery slide).

### Voice checklist (per block)
- [ ] Hero = the reader, not the brand?
- [ ] One concrete, specific claim leading?
- [ ] At least one sensory detail?
- [ ] Understated — no hype words, no "wellness," no exclamation?
- [ ] Proof (origin/process/traceability) present but secondary?
- [ ] Scarcity true, never a fake discount?
- [ ] Reads like a letter to one person?

## Applied — Products section (`#shop`)

Rewritten using the above (hero = the reader; specific + sensory leads; quiet authority via
provenance; truthful scarcity; no discounting):

- **01 · The signature tin** — H2 leads with the concrete artifact ("30g, stone-milled from the
  first harvest"); lede is sensory (jade-green, dense, quietly sweet) and ends on the reader
  ("sealed by hand … opened by you").
- **02 · The refill** — frames continuity/loss-aversion ethically ("the tin you keep stays in
  use … the morning unchanged"), not urgency.
- **03 · Provenance** — pure authority + social proof: one garden (正福茶園), three generations,
  traceable, "never blended, never anonymised," ending on the reader's certainty.

Prices unchanged (¥4,800 / ¥4,200) — anchoring is done through rarity and provenance, never discount.
