## Overall Impression

This is a confident, art-directed matcha brand homepage that has clearly improved since the last critique (29 → 32). The visual and structural craft is excellent; the gaps are now almost entirely in the *functional/trust* layer, not the design layer. The single biggest opportunity: close the above-the-fold value-proposition gap for a non-Japanese first-timer, and make the trust/help surfaces (newsletter validation, real shipping/contact info) actually work — right now the prettiest parts of the page are real and the practical parts are stubs.

## What's Working

1. **Aesthetic + minimalist design (4/4)** — The split Ken-Burns hero with the centered brush 朝虎 mark and vertical build-note (静寂の宿 / この山に建設中, index.html:55-65) is a genuine signature composition, not a template hero. Real photography throughout; no colored-block placeholders.
2. **Token-driven consistency (4/4)** — `tokens.css` is a true single source of truth (color/type/space/motion), and `base.css`/`editorial.css` reference it without hardcoding. One button system, one hairline-gold motif, AA-safe `--matcha-deep` behind white text (base.css:117). This is why it feels cohesive rather than stitched.
3. **Reduced-motion + accessibility hygiene** — Skip-link (index.html:34), `prefers-reduced-motion` gates Ken-Burns/Lenis/reveals (base.css:407, editorial.css:225, index.html:238), visible `:focus-visible` ring (base.css:42), aria-labels on icon buttons, hover-only affordances disabled on touch (`@media (hover:none)`, editorial.css:139). The motion is choreographed, not scattered.

## Priority Issues

- **[P1] Above-the-fold value proposition is unclear for a non-Japanese first-timer**
  - **What**: The hero shows imagery + vertical Japanese (静寂の宿 / この山に建設中 / 三代続く茶農家 / 鹿児島・志布志 / 新しいお茶の体験) and the brush 朝虎 mark, but no English line stating *what Asatora sells and why it's special* until the visitor scrolls into the Shop section (index.html:51-66).
  - **Why it matters**: A first-time export-market visitor (London/NY/Singapore buyers are the strategy audience) sees beauty but not the offer; the page leads with mood and defers the proposition. Bounce risk on the most expensive fold.
  - **Fix**: Add one short English value line in the hero seam-mark cluster or as the first Shop lede — e.g. "Single-origin, shade-grown matcha from one mountain in Shibushi." Keep the Japanese as the emotional layer.
  - **Suggested command**: `/impeccable clarify`

- **[P1] Newsletter form has no validation and a fake success state**
  - **What**: The submit handler unconditionally sets `.is-ok` "Welcome to the mountain." and resets, with `onsubmit="return false"` and no email check (index.html:202-206, 293). An empty or malformed address "succeeds".
  - **Why it matters**: Silent failure — a stress-tester (Riley) submits garbage and is told it worked; a real subscriber who mistypes gets no recovery. The `.is-error`/`--rust` styles exist in CSS (base.css:144-145, 338) but are never wired.
  - **Fix**: Validate the email, show the rust error state inline on failure, only show success on a real submit. Wire `aria-invalid` and `[data-error]`.
  - **Suggested command**: `/impeccable harden`

- **[P1] Footer "Help/Care" links are stubs — no real shipping, contact, or FAQ**
  - **What**: Shipping, Contact, and The Mountain Letter under "Care", plus Shop/Story/Journal under "Explore", all resolve to on-page anchors (mostly `#letter`); there is no actual help/shipping/contact content (index.html:221-222). Heuristic 10 = 2.
  - **Why it matters**: A buyer ready to purchase a ¥4,800 product with a question about shipping from Japan has nowhere to go; trust erodes at the moment of intent. For an export brand this is a conversion leak.
  - **Fix**: Add real Shipping/Contact/FAQ destinations (even a simple page or accordion), or remove the labels until they exist rather than pointing them at the newsletter.
  - **Suggested command**: `/impeccable document`

- **[P2] Add-to-cart dead-ends by design; cart count never moves**
  - **What**: Both "Add to cart" buttons open the cart drawer, which shows "Checkout opens with the store launch" (index.html:95, 104, 232). The cart badge is hardcoded `data-count="0"` and never increments.
  - **Why it matters**: A stress-tester clicks Add to cart expecting a cart action; the drawer explains the pre-launch state, which softens it, but the primary commerce CTA produces no commerce. Acceptable pre-launch, but the button label promises an action it doesn't perform.
  - **Fix**: Pre-launch, relabel to "Notify me at launch" / "Join the waitlist" wired to the newsletter, or add the item to a visible "saved" state so the click has a consequence. Keep the explanatory drawer.
  - **Suggested command**: `/impeccable harden`

- **[P2] Fraunces is an overused display face (detector + LLM agree)**
  - **What**: `--font-display` is Fraunces (tokens.css:31, loaded index.html:19) — on the reflex-reject list.
  - **Why it matters**: Caps distinctiveness; it's the one typographic choice that reads as a 2026 default. The brand has committed to it, so identity-preservation applies — but it's the most upgradeable lever if a bolder voice is wanted.
  - **Fix**: Only if pursuing a bolder identity: trial a more characterful display serif (the New Tegomin brush already gives the page its real signature; the Latin display could be braver). Otherwise leave as committed identity.
  - **Suggested command**: `/impeccable typeset`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (First-Timer)**: Lands on a gorgeous hero but the only words are Japanese (静寂の宿 / 三代続く茶農家); no English sentence says what Asatora is or sells until the Shop section. "Story", "Retreat", "Journal" nav labels are evocative but don't tell a newcomer what's behind them. Footer "Shipping"/"Contact" jump to the newsletter, not answers — Jordan's questions go unanswered.

**Riley (Stress-Tester)**: Submits the newsletter with an empty or junk email → told "Welcome to the mountain." (false success, index.html:293). Clicks "Add to cart" → drawer says checkout isn't open and cart count stays 0 (dead end, but explained). Hovers the pinned image expecting product detail; on desktop the "Hover to see it in use" crossfade works, but there's no click target to actually open a product.

**Casey (Mobile)**: Primary CTAs ("Add to cart", "Join the waitlist", "Subscribe") sit mid-page, not in a persistent thumb-zone bar — the PDP `sticky-atc` pattern exists in base.css:368 but isn't used on this homepage. Hero is `100svh` split that stacks to two rows on mobile (editorial.css:99) — needs verification that both the 朝虎 mark and the vertical build-note stay legible when stacked. Lang toggle is hidden below 768px and moved into the mobile menu (good). Touch targets meet 44px on buttons/inputs (base.css btn/input min-height 44px) — news rows are full-row anchors, fine.

## Minor Observations

- "Join the waitlist" (retreat band, index.html:178) links to `#letter`, the *newsletter* — a waitlist-vs-newsletter mismatch the visitor will notice when the form says "Harvest news and brewing notes — weekly."
- Em-dash count is up to ~22 occurrences, but most are the decorative news-row `—` arrow (CSS `::after` content) and the band heading `<br>`; only a couple are in body voice. Lower concern than the prior run implied, but the news-row arrow reinforces the em-dash motif as a recurring device.
- The detector's `numbered-section-markers` hit is a false positive against this page (field-report catalog numbers + dates, not section eyebrows).
- Two `<button>` "Add to cart" instances share `data-cart-open` with the cart icon — semantically the same action; fine, but the label sets a different expectation than the icon.
- `index.html.pre-yym.bak` (16KB) sits in the repo root — a stray backup that should be gitignored or removed (housekeeping, not design).

## Questions to Consider

- What if the hero carried one decisive English line — would the Japanese mood actually read *stronger* once a newcomer knows what they're looking at?
- Does the newsletter need to pretend to succeed? A real validation + error state costs little and removes the only place the page lies to the user.
- If the store isn't live, should the primary CTA be "Add to cart" at all — or is the honest, higher-converting pre-launch action "Join the waitlist"?
- The page's real signature is the brush 朝虎 mark and vertical Japanese, not Fraunces. What would a version that leaned *harder* into the brush/vertical voice and *quieter* on the Latin display look like?
